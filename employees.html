URL: https://www.reuters.com/business/world-at-work/half-americans-fear-ai-could-put-someone-their-household-out-work-reutersipsos-2026-06-10/

This article reports survey findings showing widespread fear amongst American workers that AI could put them or someone in their household out of work. It highlights emotional and economic anxieties, making it a valuable source for understanding how employees perceive the issue of AI driven job displacement. The article is especially useful for readers exploring the public sentiment surrounding labor market instability. It also provides real world data rather than mere speculation. Limitations include its focus on American respondents rather than worldwide respondents, as well as its reliance on survey answers rather than actual displacement statistics. Still, it offers a grounded view of employee concerns during a period of rapid AI adoption in the workplace.

URL: https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2025-ai-index-report

This report provides a detailed overview of global AI trends, particularly regarding their impact on the labor market. The report synthesizes qualitative and quantitative data to show how the rapid advancement in AI correlates with the state of employment. This source's research driven approach and the credibility of Stanford University make it valuable for understanding how employees are experiencing and anticipating AI driven disruption to the workforce. Researchers, policymakers, and workers seeking evidence-based insight into the impacts of AI on the job market would benefit most from reading this source. A limitation is that the report's broad scope can make employee-specific concerns less detailed, and its projections may become quickly outdated as AI continues to rapidly advance.

This article discusses Goldman Sachs' projection that generative AI could automate up to 300 million jobs globally, detailing which industries face the highest risk of human worker displacement. It frames AI as both a threat to job security and a catalyst for economic growth, offering quantitative data that help convey employee concerns. The source's value comes from its economic modeling, which makes it helpful for understanding the large-scale impacts of AI on the workforce. Workers, students, and researchers studying automation trends would benefit most from going through this article. A limitation of the source is that its focus on long-range forecasts is inherently uncertain and could become quickly outdated, and the analysis may be biased towards economic efficiency rather than human costs.
