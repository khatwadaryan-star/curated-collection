URL: https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/

This jobs report by the World Economic Forum provides global data on how governments and policymakers are anticipating labor market changes due to AI. It includes projections on job creation, job loss, reskilling needs, and regulatory challenges. This source is valuable because it offers an international and policy-oriented analysis into this issue, making it ideal for readers who are interested in the government responses to technological disruption. Its strengths include comprehensive data and comparisons between countries, as well as the high level of credibility that comes with an organization like the World Economic Forum. A limitation is its forward-looking nature, as the projections may not fully match future outcomes and thus could become outdated quickly.

URL: https://yjil.yale.edu/posts/2025-11-10-ai-job-displacement-and-the-wto-identifying-legal-gaps-and-charting-a-worker

This legal analysis explores how governments and international institutions should respond to AI-driven job displacement, with a focus on the World Trade Organization (WTO). It argues that existing trade rules fail to address labor disruptions caused by automation and proposes new policies to fix this. The source's value lies in the credibility of Yale University, as well as its unique international perspective, making it ideal for understanding how governments could respond to and regulate the economic effects of AI adoption in the workforce. Policy analysts, legal scholars, and government officials would benefit most from reading this analysis. A limitation of the source is its detailed legal framing, which may require prior background knowledge, as well as its bias towards an anti-AI narrative.

URL: https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2026/03/ai-jobs-training-workplace-governments-policy-response

This report from Stanford University summarizes two Hoover Institution panels where scholars, industry leaders, and former policymakers discuss how governments should respond to AI-driven workplace disruption. It covers declining employment in highly automated job categories and the inefficiency of current reskilling systems. It also highlights proposed policy responses such as flexible lifelong learning programs. This source is valuable because it provides a clear window into government perspectives on AI displacement, emphasizing their urgency of updating labor market support systems while supporting economic growth from AI. People who would find this report useful include researchers or students studying government AI policy, public administrators, and labor economists. Limitations of this article include its emphasis on viewpoints from a specific set of experts, which may not represent the overall viewpoint of governments, as well as the focus on the U.S. and the U.K., limiting this article's generalization to other countries.
